L = 40.0 × √(1 − 0.600²) = 40.0 × 0.800 = 32.0 m
Same answer the short way: L = L₀ ÷ γ = 40.0 ÷ 1.250 = 32.0 m

How L shrinks as speed climbs

Nearly flat at everyday speeds, then it falls off a cliff.

Only along the direction of travel

The rocket's height never changes, at any speed. Contraction happens along the line of motion and nowhere else — check the vertical marker as you push the slider.

Misconception alert

Nothing squeezes the rocket. No force presses on the hull, no part is under stress, and the crew feels nothing unusual.

Length is not a property the rocket carries around. It is the result of a measurement, and the answer depends on who is measuring.

For your future classroom

Ask the class: which of the two is really shorter? Let them argue, then show that both frames are equally valid — the crew measures the ground contracted by exactly the same factor.

The question has no frame-free answer. That is the lesson, not a loophole.

The motion here is slowed far below scale so it can be seen. At 0.6c a real rocket would cross this stretch of ground in under a microsecond.
