Time dilation

Two identical light clocks. One stays home, one rides the rocket. Watch the tick counters drift apart.

Sci 315 Modern PhysicsUnit I: Relativity

Ground frame

your clock on the left, the rocket's clock flying past

The pulse in the moving clock has farther to travel, but light cannot travel any faster. Something has to give, and it is the time between ticks.

Rocket frame

the crew's clock is the still one now

Nothing aboard has changed. The crew's pulse still goes straight up and down, and now it is the ground clock that falls behind.

How γ climbs with speed

Barely above 1 for anything you can drive, then it runs away.

Not a quirk of light clocks

The light clock is a thinking tool, not a special device. Atomic clocks, chemical reactions, decaying particles, and heartbeats all run slow by the same factor γ.

If they did not, the crew could compare two clocks aboard and work out their own absolute speed — and that is exactly what Einstein's first postulate forbids.

Misconception alert

The crew never feels time crawl. One second aboard feels like one second, and their clock keeps perfect time against everything else on the rocket.

"Moving clocks run slow" is always shorthand for slow as measured from the other frame. Drop that ending and the sentence stops being true.

For your future classroom

Both panels are correct, and students will notice. Each frame measures the other's clock as the slow one, because "at the same time" means something different in each. Park the argument here and pick it up in the twin paradox.
